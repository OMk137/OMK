Famous Experiments in Quantum Physics

Quantum physics is not just theory. These strange ideas have been proven again and again through experiments. Here are some of the most important ones.

  1. The Double Slit Experiment

    This is one of the most famous experiments in all of physics. When scientists fire particles like electrons or photons at a barrier with two slits, the particles form a wave pattern on the screen behind, as if each particle passed through both slits at once like a wave.

    But here is the twist. If you set up a detector to watch which slit the particle goes through, the wave pattern disappears and the particles behave like normal solid objects. Just the act of observing changes the result.

  2. Schrodinger's Cat (Thought Experiment)

    Physicist Erwin Schrodinger created this mind bending thought experiment in 1935 to show how strange quantum superposition is. Imagine a cat in a sealed box with a radioactive atom, a detector, and poison that is released if the atom decays. Quantum theory says the atom can be both decayed and not decayed until it is observed, which means the cat would be both alive and dead at the same time.

    Of course, in reality, we do not put cats in boxes like this. It is just an illustration of how unusual quantum rules are.

  3. The Stern Gerlach Experiment

    In 1922, Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach passed silver atoms through a magnetic field. Instead of spreading out smoothly, the atoms split into two distinct beams. This showed that a property of particles called spin comes in fixed amounts (quantized) rather than any random value.

    It was one of the first clear signs that nature works in discrete steps at the quantum level.
