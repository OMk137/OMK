Core Concepts of Quantum Physics

Quantum physics is full of ideas that sound impossible at first. These are the main concepts you need to understand to get a feel for how the quantum world works.

  1. Wave Particle Duality

    In everyday life, things are either particles (like pebbles) or waves (like ripples in water). But in the quantum world, particles like electrons and photons can act like both.

    Example: In the famous double slit experiment, when electrons are fired at two slits, they create a wave pattern even though they're particles. This means they can behave like waves of probability until we observe them.

  2. Superposition

    A quantum particle can be in more than one state at the same time like a coin being both heads and tails until you look.

    Example: In quantum computing, a qubit can be 0 and 1 at the same time, allowing for massive computing power.

  3. Quantum Entanglement

    Two particles can become linked so that the state of one instantly affects the other, no matter how far apart they are. If you change one, the other reacts immediately even across galaxies.

    Einstein called it spooky action at a distance, because it seemed to break the rule that nothing can travel faster than light.

  4. Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle

    You can't know both the exact position and the exact momentum (speed and direction) of a particle at the same time.

    This isn't because our tools are bad, it's a built-in rule of nature. The more precisely you know one value, the fuzzier the other becomes.

  5. Quantum Tunneling

    Sometimes, particles can pass through barriers that they shouldn't be able to, according to classical physics.

    Example: Imagine rolling a ball at a hill that's too high for it to climb. In quantum physics, there's a chance the ball could simply appear on the other side of the hill without ever going over the top. This is how some electronics and nuclear reactions work.
