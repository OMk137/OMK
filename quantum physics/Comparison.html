Quantum vs Classical Physics

Classical physics is the set of rules that explains most of the world we see and touch every day. It comes from the work of scientists like Isaac Newton and James Clerk Maxwell. In classical physics, objects have definite positions and speeds. If you know all the starting information, you can predict exactly what will happen. A ball thrown into the air will follow a predictable path. A planet will move in its orbit according to clear laws of gravity.

Quantum physics is different. It describes the behavior of particles that are extremely small, like electrons, photons, and atoms. In the quantum world, certainty is replaced by probability. Instead of saying exactly where a particle is, quantum theory can only tell us the chances of finding it in a certain place. Particles can be in multiple states at once, and observing them changes how they behave.

Another big difference is scale. Classical physics works well for large objects, like cars, people, and planets. Quantum physics rules the tiny world of atoms and subatomic particles. At that level, many of the common sense rules of classical physics simply break down.

The two sets of rules are not in conflict, they are just used for different situations. Classical physics is actually an approximation of quantum physics that works when things are big enough for quantum effects to fade away.
